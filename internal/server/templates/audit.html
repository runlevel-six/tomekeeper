Bodies worth a look

Needs attention lists what did not arrive. This asks the harder question underneath it — what arrived and is wrong — through three lenses over the bodies you read. A cookie notice, a sign-in wall or a related-articles rail reads as prose and passes every threshold extraction enforces, so nothing but a second look finds one.

{{/* Said before the findings rather than after them, because a list of complaints with no stated error rate reads as a list of faults. Measured on a real archive, the first lens flagged seven bodies and not one was a body extraction had got wrong. That is a fine list to read and a terrible one to act on automatically, which is why nothing here changes anything. */}} Expect false alarms. Every lens is deliberately imprecise: a link roundup, a podcast episode page or a title in another language will show up here and be perfectly fine. Nothing on this page has been changed, and nothing will be unless you ask.

Bodies that say nothing their title mentions

Extraction chose a block of the page and may have chosen the wrong one. A body sharing no distinctive word with its own title is the shape that catches it — a consent dialog has nothing to say about the article it is covering. Only the two rungs that choose a block are examined; a hand-written selector cannot wander, and a comic legitimately has no prose to match.

Bodies more than one article shares

Two articles do not coincidentally have the same prose. What they share is usually a wall — a consent gate, a sign-in page, an "enable JavaScript" notice — stored as though it were the article. Reposts of the same story are the ordinary innocent case. This one finds a pattern only after it has repeated, which is what an audit is for.

Titles that are not titles

A URL or an encoded filename kept as a title, which an import does when its source had none for a bookmark. Both halves that caused it are fixed, so this finds the ones already stored — and the two remedies are not interchangeable: one of these has a stored page to take a real title from, and one has nothing to read a title out of and needs a fetch first.
